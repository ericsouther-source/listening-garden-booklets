NOTES

Over the past six months, I have developed Listening Garden, a sonic playground shaped by my listening and reflection. Working with third-order ambisonics, I explore how sounds move, gather, and interact in the space around me.

I use biological statistics to guide how sounds evolve, alongside annotations from my own listening. This accumulated activity feeds the algorithmic arrangement of sounds, carrying a history of exploration into each new composition.

The Garden’s FX organisms, sound processors with their own behaviors, and its Mycelium network extend this exploration in real time. Through these connections, the activity of one sound can shape how another is processed.

I keep returning to software as a signal: code accumulating like sediment, becoming an information processor much like an audio processor. What happens when software development approaches the speed of an oscillator? These systems can turn musical knowledge into working tools quickly, but I still decide what to hear, keep, and change.
