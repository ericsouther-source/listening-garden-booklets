Feral Current.

How a gathered sound library, six algorithmic scores and human listening became an album.

Feral Current gathers six months of playing with sound into six tracks, drawing on 809 distinct sounds from a 1,000-sound pool in Listening Garden. This sedimentation of practice takes new form through algorithmic composition with an AI collaborator. I guided the exploration through artistic direction and listening; together we developed rules and scores that the Garden’s audio engine performed. Garden-processed sounds meet in rhythms that dissolve and return, bringing accumulated experiments into unexpected relationships.

01 / THE CAST

A pool, then a cast.

The 1,000-sound pool was an invitation to choose, not an instruction to use everything. Across the six pieces, 809 distinct selected recordings entered the scores. That number counts used recordings; it does not mean 809 independent original recordings.

The cast has a mixed ancestry. It includes Garden-processed descendants of material made with Stable Audio 3 and ElevenLabs SFX, alongside other sounds gathered and changed through the Garden. Their prior histories matter because curation begins before any score selects a sound.

02 / THE RULES

Give the music a way to leave.

The artistic direction asked for a recognizable rhythmic floor that could break apart and become legible again. An AI collaborator planned six unequal track scores around that brief: shifting clocks, short motif memories, interruptions, grain clouds and changed returns. The native Rust/DSP engine performed those saved scores. The separate production Composer was not used for this album.

A rule is audible here through its consequences rather than as an explanation to decode. A beat can disappear by score while an echo or small interval remains as a thread. When the pulse returns, the surrounding register, accent or material may have moved.

03 / THE FIELD

Space is part of the score.

The scores place sound events in a shared third-order ambisonic field. They use moving stereo grain pairs, echoes and spatial filtering as compositional controls. A fragment can be carried into another part of that field while another event holds the rhythmic floor. The spatial design helps the music separate, cross and return; it is more than a finish applied after the arrangement.

Listening prompt: after a dense passage, attend to where the next stable beat appears in relation to the fragments around it.
04 / LISTENING BACK

The ear makes decisions too.

Eric directed the brief, listened to the results and revised the direction. In feedback on Low Water Memory, he singled out its beat anchor as a reason the piece worked for him. That is a specific listening judgment, not a claim that every passage reached a settled form.

The process distributes work across curation, score planning, engine performance and later listening. None of those steps replaces the others. The question for each revision is whether the score leaves enough trace for a return to feel earned.

05 / HISTORICAL CONNECTIONS

Two nearby questions.

Pierre Schaeffer’s Treatise on Musical Objects treats recorded sound and listening as compositional material. It offers a comparison for the attention paid here to what a sound becomes once it is selected and processed. Read about Schaeffer’s primary text ↗

In a 1995 interview, Brian Eno described building musical systems, then paying close attention to the sonic material he fed into them. Feral Current poses a related question about where composition sits when a score directs a process and a person listens back. These are comparisons for thinking with the album, not claims of direct influence. Read Eno’s interview ↗

06 / NEXT QUESTIONS

What should the next listening test?

The strongest question may be how little needs to remain for a return to be recognized. A motif can survive as an interval, a timing gesture or an echo while its original setting disappears.

  • Which returns are clear because of the rhythm, and which because of a smaller remembered contour?
  • When does a spatial movement change the musical relationship rather than simply decorate it?
  • What would a second cast of sounds reveal about the same rules?
