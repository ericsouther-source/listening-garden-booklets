DRAFT FOR REVIEW

Feral Current.

How a gathered sound library, six algorithmic scores and human listening became an album.

Feral Current gathers six months of playing with sound into six tracks, drawing on 809 distinct sounds from a 1,000-sound pool in Listening Garden. This sedimentation of practice takes new form through algorithmic composition with an AI collaborator. I guided the exploration through artistic direction and listening; together we developed rules and scores that the Garden’s audio engine performed. Garden-processed sounds meet in rhythms that dissolve and return, bringing accumulated experiments into unexpected relationships.

01 / THE CAST

A pool, then a cast.

The selected pool offered 1,000 Listening Garden candidates, rather than a quota to use every sound. The score audit counts 809 distinct sound identities used across the six pieces. It verifies that count within these arrangements, not 809 independently original sources.

The cast has mixed ancestry. Some used sounds have recorded links to Stable Audio 3 or ElevenLabs SFX material, and some have incomplete ancestry. Curation therefore begins before the album’s rules select a sound.

02 / THE RULES

Give the music a way to leave.

My brief called for dance rhythms that could lose their legible form and return. An AI collaborator planned six scores of unequal duration around that request. The score files specify crossing clocks, remembered motifs and brief clouds of linked grain pairs. The Garden’s native Rust/DSP engine performed them.

The sections mark when the pulse holds, fractures or disappears by score. Motif returns keep recorded interval and onset patterns but can be transposed; echoes and other parts can carry motion between sections. A return is planned as a changed relationship, rather than a reset to the opening.

Process note: The separate production Composer was not used to make this album.

03 / THE FIELD

Space is part of the score.

The six saved scores place sounds in a shared third-order ambisonic field. Their controls include moving stereo grain trajectories, timed echoes and spatial EQ. Those positions and movements are written into the performance plan: they shape how layers meet and separate as the rhythms change.

Listening prompt: after a dense passage, attend to where the next stable beat appears in relation to the fragments around it.
04 / LISTENING BACK

The ear makes decisions too.

I set the direction and listened to the result. In later feedback, I identified the beat anchor—particularly in Low Water Memory—as one reason Feral Current works for me. That note points to a concrete musical choice; it is not a verdict on every passage.

The process distributes work across curation, score planning, engine performance and listening back. The open question for another revision is whether enough of a rhythm or contour remains for its return to register.

05 / HISTORICAL CONNECTIONS

Two nearby questions.

Pierre Schaeffer’s Treatise on Musical Objects treats recorded sound and listening as compositional material. It offers a comparison for the attention paid here to what a sound becomes once it is selected and processed. Read about Schaeffer’s primary text ↗

In a 1995 interview, Brian Eno described building musical systems, then paying close attention to the sonic material he fed into them. Feral Current poses a related question about where composition sits when a score directs a process and a person listens back. These are comparisons for thinking with the album, not claims of direct influence. Read Eno’s interview ↗

06 / NEXT QUESTIONS

What should the next listening test?

The strongest question may be how little needs to remain for a return to be recognized. A motif can survive as an interval, a timing gesture or an echo while its original setting disappears.

  • Which returns are clear because of the rhythm, and which because of a smaller remembered contour?
  • When does a spatial movement change the musical relationship rather than simply decorate it?
  • What would a second cast of sounds reveal about the same rules?
