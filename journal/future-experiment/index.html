NOTES FROM THE GARDEN / 02

DRAFT FOR REVIEW

A garden for
the next years.

A future experiment in making sounds, hearing them again, and letting that listening shape composition.

PROPOSAL / OPEN QUESTIONSERIC SOUTHER

In this entry
  1. 01 / The material
  2. 02 / A listening record
  3. 03 / The question

I want to start another garden and stay with it for years. Its source sounds would come from my synthesizer and modular practice. I would bring those recordings in, transform them, compose with them, and keep returning to what I hear. The older garden would remain a history beside this new one.

01 / THE MATERIAL

Make the sound, then meet it again.

For this particular experiment, I want the source audio to be made with my instruments, without model-generated audio. I am interested in what happens when a sound I made moves through different treatments and musical roles. Is it still recognizable? Does it begin to suggest a new direction when it meets other sounds?

I imagine an editable, multitrack space where the origin of a sound and its later uses remain connected. I want room for composition and surprise, rather than a system that only repeats what I already know I like. This is a plan, not a finished Garden feature.

02 / A LISTENING RECORD

Let the words stay close to the sound.

As I listen, I want to speak annotations tied to the moments that prompted them. The microphone stays on after the piece ends so I can speak a separate reflection while the listening is still present. A first reaction, a later thought, and the sound itself should remain distinct but linked. An uncertain or changed opinion matters too.

Some of my working words may be Moss, Bloom, Roots or Vine. Other descriptions will be less tidy. I want a vocabulary that can grow from what I actually say, including the difference between what I hoped a sound would do and what it did when I heard it.

03 / THE QUESTION

Can practice improve the composer?

AI may help me build the tools, and I may test local models separately. During composition, I want to explore how much can live in the Garden’s own rules and audio engine, with less reliance on frontier models. A listening record would be evidence for that work, not by itself a learning composer. I do not yet know what balance will work.

Over one to three years, or longer, I want to ask whether sustained making and listening can improve the Garden’s algorithmic composer. Could it approach the musical sophistication of pieces I have made with frontier models helping plan the composition? That is a comparison to test, not a promise of parity. Can the Garden remember a preference without flattening a surprise? Can I hear a real change in my own taste, or am I hearing a changed sound in a changed context? Those answers will have to come from making and listening over time.
