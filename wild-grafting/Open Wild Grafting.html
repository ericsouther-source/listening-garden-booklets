HOW A LIBRARY BECOMES MUSIC

A sound arrives with a history.

A musical role begins with a need: hold a line, give a gesture weight, answer with an edge. The library brings a sound with its own attack, grain and internal movement. That choice starts to shape the piece before the sound is transformed.

“I'm fascinated by the unexpected combinations and unlikely paths through which the sample library answers the composition's musical roles.”

Listening Garden

There is room for chance, but also for listening and selection. A surprising candidate can change the character of a phrase. Its timing, articulation and place in the field then help it take on a musical role.

Same material, another way of speaking.

In these two passages, a shared source appears in contrasting roles. Listen for a family resemblance between the brief, broken gestures and the longer lines.

Look closer: what is shared?

These gestures use the same library material within each pair. The longer gesture is not a recording of the shorter one stretched out: each is made separately for its place in the phrase. Kinship can survive a change in pitch, length and touch.

A closer look at the relationship

Every drawing uses the same pitch scale. The entrances keep their relative positions in time, even when the final phrase takes half as long. The selector changes the drawing only.

Keeping the direction of each step does not keep every relationship between the pitches. That is one way the phrase can stay familiar while becoming less predictable.

What does the sphere show?

These are the directions written into the arrangement for five selected voices. Each linked pair of dots shows the two sides of a stereo source. The dotted trail follows its recent movement.

The sphere is a map of the composition, not a measurement of where you will hear a sound. Distance from the center does not mean loudness or distance from the listener. A note’s tail can continue after its marker opens.

The camera begins at a fixed angle. Turning it changes your view of the drawing, not the binaural recording.

Look closer: what does binaural mean here?

The album was composed in a three-dimensional sound field. Its released two-channel mix carries a headphone rendering of that field. You can play it as an ordinary stereo recording; headphones keep the left- and right-ear signals separate.

The drawing shows the arrangement's stored directions. It helps explain how the voices relate in space; the sense of location you hear will also depend on your headphones and your own hearing.

04

BOW TO BURR / SHADE AT FULL SPEED

Speed can leave room.

Fast activity does not have to take over the whole space. In Bow to Burr, the quiet, fast passage asks for attention through detail. In Shade at Full Speed, a brief forceful moment gains its setting from the time around it.

Keep a comfortable volume across both excerpts. Notice how much is happening, and how much room the piece still gives you.

COMPANION ALBUM / SIX PIECES / 30 MINUTES

Wild Grafting
with the Garden Voices

One composition, another cast. These six pieces return to the album’s original score, drawing from a different collection of appropriated short samples, many containing voices. New sounds take on the same musical roles.

The written notes, timing and paths through the ambisonic field stay in place. Each new sound is shaped to fit its part, bringing a different grain, attack and sense of weight. Listen for what survives the change of material: a familiar contour, an uneven step, a breath between gestures.
